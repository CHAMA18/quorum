# Quorum - teleprompter

A hospital cannot use equipment that is still waiting for procurement review. Across Africa, public procurement averages seventeen percent of GDP, according to the World Bank. In Zambia, Quorum starts with the officer's desk: read the evidence, identify the gaps, and prepare a decision that people can explain.

Here is the actual supplier workflow. Required documents stay together in one checklist. The supplier opens a supporting file and requests an AI document check. The findings appear beside the source, so the reviewer can compare them. This helps prepare the submission; it does not decide whether the supplier qualifies.

Now the recorded officer workspace. The team moves from the tender notice through opening and evaluation. Published criteria govern the review. Officers can inspect the matrix, compare the admitted bids, and trace the supporting file. These earlier recordings use illustrative records; their fields vary. The committee enters its own scores. Quorum keeps the review and the accountable decision connected.

The officer opens the AI-assisted bid review, confirms permission to share the documents, and starts the assessment. Every admitted bidder is considered against the same issued criteria. Watch what happens when the submitted information does not establish a requirement: the result says, not evidenced. The AI leaves the gap visible and gives the committee questions to resolve. It can offer an advisory comparison, but it cannot turn missing evidence into a pass. Muse Spark on Meta Model API also returned the separately verified source-linked assessment included with this submission.

The original recording also shows the people who make the decision. Here is the committee panel, followed by member registration. Each officer has an identity, role and recorded position. Evaluator sheets, score differences and committee consensus remain part of the file. The AI's recommendation is advice for consideration; official scores, formal ranking and the award remain with authorised people. Quorum keeps that distinction visible.

We also tested the approval boundary against the production build. An unsigned request returns four oh one. A signed-in request without step-up verification returns four oh three. The gate remains pending with no receipt. A separate authorised fictional account then completes two-factor verification, and the local simulation produces a signature-verified receipt. This is test evidence, not a government portal transaction.
